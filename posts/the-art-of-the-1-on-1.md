# The Art of the 1-on-1: Managing Beyond Status Updates

Many managers treat 1-on-1 meetings with team members as an informal project sync. They go through Jira tickets, ask about delivery dates, and discuss bugs. 

That is what stand-ups and slack channels are for. Your 1-on-1 time belongs entirely to the individual's growth, mindset, and career vector.

## Flip the Ownership
The report should own the agenda of the meeting, not the manager. Give them an open shared document to drop items into throughout the week. If they show up with no agenda, ask foundational questions:
- *What felt unexpectedly frustrating or draining this week?*
- *Who on the team went out of their way to help you out?*
- *Do you clear see how your current tasks map to your long-term career progression goals?*

## Listen for the Silences
Pay attention to energy changes, subtle frustrations, and what they choose *not* to talk about. A great manager uses 1-on-1 sessions to build enough mutual psychological safety that real blockages can be surfaced long before they turn into resignation letters.
