# Product Thinking for Engineers

There's a common career inflection point for engineers: the moment you realize that "build what you're told" is a ceiling, not a floor.

The engineers who break through that ceiling have one thing in common: they think about users.

## The Specification Trap

A spec is someone's best guess about what will solve a problem. It's not the problem itself. When you only execute against specs, you're one step removed from the actual work of building products.

The question "should I build this?" is at least as important as "how should I build this?"

## Developing Product Instincts

Product intuition isn't a personality trait. It's a skill you develop by:

1. **Using your own product obsessively** — You cannot have good instincts about things you don't use
2. **Reading support tickets** — Users tell you exactly what's broken if you listen
3. **Watching real users** — Not usability studies, just someone trying to use your product for the first time

## The Leverage Point

The engineer who can say "I don't think we should build this because..." and be right more often than not has enormous leverage. They're not just executing. They're participating in product decisions.

That participation is available to everyone. It just requires asking questions one level up from the implementation.
