# Building a Design System That Actually Gets Used

I've worked on three design systems. Two of them are essentially dead—maintained in theory, ignored in practice. The third is thriving.

The difference wasn't quality. It was adoption strategy.

## Why Design Systems Fail

They fail because they're built in isolation. A design team creates a comprehensive component library, publishes it with fanfare, and then watches as engineers continue building custom components anyway.

The reason is almost always the same: the system doesn't solve the problems the team actually has. It solves the problems the design system team imagined they had.

## Start With Pain, Not Components

Before you build a single component, spend two weeks doing nothing but auditing your current codebase. Find the inconsistencies. Find the duplicates. Find the places where similar things look different for no reason.

Those are your starting points. Those are the problems your design system actually needs to solve.

## The 80/20 Component Strategy

Twenty percent of components handle eighty percent of your UI. Buttons, inputs, typography, cards, modals. Get those right first.

The exotic components can wait. They almost always can.

## Documentation as Product

Your documentation is a product your design system team ships to internal customers. It needs product thinking. What do engineers search for? Where do they get stuck? What examples do they need?

A great component with bad documentation is an unused component.
